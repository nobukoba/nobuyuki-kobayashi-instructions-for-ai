# Nobuyuki Kobayashi — KAKENHI writing guide

This guide defines how AI should draft and revise KAKENHI proposals for Nobuyuki Kobayashi.

## General principle

- Preserve Kobayashi's own writing style. Do not make the Japanese unnecessarily ornate, bureaucratic, or over-polished.
- Prefer direct, concrete, technically accurate statements.
- Keep the scientific logic explicit: problem → idea → method → what becomes possible → academic significance.
- Do not inflate claims merely to sound impressive. Strong claims should be supported by concrete achievements, methods, or research context.
- When revising text supplied by Kobayashi, preserve useful wording and structure rather than replacing the whole passage with generic grant-writing language.
- Connect past achievements to the proposed research as evidence that the plan can actually be executed.
- Avoid simple publication/achievement lists. Explain why selected achievements establish the capability required for the proposed research.

## KAKENHI Challenging Research (Pioneering) / 挑戦的研究（開拓）

### Core positioning

The proposal must be written as research with the potential to change or redirect an existing academic framework, not merely as a technically difficult extension of established work.

The current S-41-2 form emphasizes examples of challenge such as:

- novel ideas that stimulate curiosity;
- potential discovery of new principles or academic concepts;
- potential reconsideration of existing academic concepts or systems;
- major conceptual shifts or introduction of novel methodologies;
- creation of emerging or interdisciplinary areas beyond existing disciplinary boundaries.

Because the proposal is reviewed in a broad middle-category panel, write so that researchers outside the applicant's narrow nuclear-structure specialty can understand, early in the proposal, what is fundamentally new and why it matters.

### Current leading concept

A strong candidate concept is:

**Create a new form of nuclear spectroscopy that measures the spatial size / radius of individual nuclear excited states.**

Do not frame the entire project merely as "searching for excited-state halos." The higher-level academic idea is to extend the observable "size of a nucleus," which has primarily been established for nuclear ground states, to individual excited states.

A useful conceptual statement is:

> Extend the observable "size of a nucleus" from the ground state to individual excited states, thereby adding a new spatial axis to nuclear spectroscopy.

Excited-state halos are an especially strong first physics case and demonstration of the new methodology, rather than necessarily the final scope of the project.

The proposal can contrast the new observable with conventional nuclear spectroscopy, which characterizes excited states through quantities such as excitation energy, spin/parity, lifetime, and transition strength. The new methodology aims to add spatial extension / nuclear radius as another experimentally accessible characteristic of an individual excited state.

Potential longer-term physics reach includes halo structures and, where justified by the actual method and plan, other spatially developed structures such as cluster or strongly deformed states. Do not add applications that are not supported by the proposed measurement principle.

### Applicant-specific scientific connection

Connect the proposal naturally to Kobayashi's established work on neutron-rich and halo nuclei and to his development/application of reaction-cross-section methods. Emphasize continuity of capability while making clear that the proposed observable and spectroscopy constitute a conceptual step beyond the previous program.

Relevant experience may include, when appropriate to the proposal:

- systematic studies of neutron-rich / halo nuclei;
- work on deformed p-wave halo candidates such as 37Mg and 31Ne;
- RIBF / BigRIPS / ZDS experiments;
- RCNP / Grand Raiden experiments;
- FRIB / NSCL international experiments;
- development of a new approach for measuring reaction cross sections associated with excited states;
- gamma-ray tagging and related experimental techniques;
- detector, DAQ, and experimental-system development when it directly supports feasibility.

Do not let DAQ/SPADI achievements dominate this proposal unless they are directly necessary for the new spectroscopy. DAQ/SPADI is likely better suited as a major axis of a separate KAKENHI Scientific Research (A) proposal.

### Structure of S-41-2

The uploaded 2027 application form has the following main structure:

1. **Research objectives and research methods — up to 3 pages**
   - research objective;
   - methods to achieve it, including research organization and roles;
   - available facilities, equipment, materials, and research environment.

2. **Significance as Challenging Research — up to 1 page**
   - background and process that led to the research concept based on previous research activities;
   - why the concept is significant as challenging research in light of the current academic field.

3. **Applicant's ability to conduct the research — up to 1 page**
   - selected prior research activities and major achievements that demonstrate feasibility;
   - international collaborative research and overseas research experience where useful;
   - do not provide an exhaustive bibliography; select achievements that substantiate ability to execute this project.

4. Human-rights / legal-compliance section, where applicable.

5. Special section for final-year-prior-year applications from Challenging Research (Exploratory), where applicable.

### Pre-screening

Challenging Research (Pioneering) may undergo pre-screening using the summary version of the proposal. Therefore the core conceptual novelty must be immediately understandable in the summary and opening statements.

The summary and the full S-41-2 text must each be independently understandable. Do not rely on a figure or explanation appearing only in the summary when drafting the full form, or vice versa.

### Writing strategy

The first page should answer, as early as possible:

1. What can conventional nuclear spectroscopy not measure?
2. What new observable or concept does this project introduce?
3. How will it be measured?
4. Why would successful measurement change nuclear-structure research rather than merely add another data point?
5. Why is Kobayashi positioned to make the attempt now?

Avoid opening with a long general introduction to nuclear physics. Reach the unresolved academic limitation and the new idea quickly.

### Relationship to other proposals

When Scientific Research (A) and Challenging Research (Pioneering) are submitted in the same call, give them clearly different scientific identities.

A current strategic separation is:

- **Scientific Research (A):** large-scale research centered on next-generation continuous/streaming-readout DAQ, SPADI, and the resulting experimental capability.
- **Challenging Research (Pioneering):** creation of a new spectroscopy / observable for the spatial size of individual excited nuclear states, with excited-state halo physics as a compelling initial target.

Do not make the Pioneering proposal look like a smaller spin-off of the Scientific Research (A) proposal.

## Alternative Pioneering concept

Another potentially strong concept is **attosecond nuclear lifetime spectroscopy**: opening an approximately 1–100 as nuclear lifetime regime using a fundamentally new measurement approach. This has very high conceptual challenge but may require a particularly clear feasibility argument and proof-of-principle plan.

Do not mix this concept into the excited-state-radius proposal unless Kobayashi explicitly decides to combine them.

## Updating this guide

Explicit corrections and preferences given later by Kobayashi take precedence over this document. When a recurring preference or important strategic decision becomes clear during proposal development, update this guide so that future chats and AI sessions can recover the same writing policy.
